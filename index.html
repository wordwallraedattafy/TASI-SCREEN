Notes

Notes. The "Daily data file" source reads data/tasi_data.json, which the automation script refreshes after each trading day, so scans take seconds and need no relays. Price alerts: prices come from Yahoo Finance and can be delayed (often about 15 minutes), so an alert fires when the delayed price passes your level. Between two checks the tool also looks at the day's high and low, so a quick move through your price is not missed. Alerts only run while this page is open. Notifications are sent from this page, so they only work while it is open. Browser notifications may be blocked when the file is opened from your disk; ntfy, Telegram and Discord are more reliable. Anyone who knows your ntfy topic name can read your alerts, so use a long random name. Fibonacci: the tool takes the highest high and lowest low of the swing window (default 120 days; the swing must be at least the minimum size). If the low came after the high, it levels the rebound at 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% of that drop and lists stocks whose close just crossed above the level. With "Also show crosses below" ticked, it also lists pullbacks that crossed down through a level after a rally. Opening a Fibonacci row's chart draws the swing levels. The market overview covers only the stocks you scanned, so scan the full list for a true picture; "last session" is the most recent trading day in the data. Click the chart button in a row to open a 120-day chart with the 50 and 200-day averages and the signal date. "Vol x" is the volume on the signal day divided by the average of the previous 20 days; 1.5x or more is marked as high, and high volume makes a signal more meaningful. "NEW" marks signals that were not in the last completed scan on this computer; the first scan has nothing to compare with. RSI uses Wilder's method on daily closes (period and level are adjustable; default 14 and 50). For RSI and moving-average rows, "VsReference" in the CSV is the distance from the average (%) or the points above the RSI level. Open this file from your computer in a browser. A "new low" means the day's lowest price fell below every earlier low in the window: the previous 252 trading days for the 52-week low, and the full available history for the all-time low. "All-time" only means as far back as the source's data goes (shown in the "Data since" column), and a stock needs at least a year of history to qualify. Prices are not adjusted for stock splits or capital changes, so an old split can create a false all-time low; check the chart through the links. Speed: the scan first loads 2 years of prices per stock and downloads the full history only for stocks at a new 52-week low; raise "Parallel requests" for speed, but too many can make the free relays refuse requests. Yahoo requests go through public CORS relays and can be slow or blocked. The two Yahoo options share the same data; only Twelve Data is independent. Data can be delayed. This is a screening aid, not investment advice.
